How the peg pyramid works

The board is built as a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row one peg wider than the row above. A ball released at the top meets a near-even choice left or right at every peg it touches. That same split repeats row after row until the ball lands in one of the pockets at the bottom, which are arranged as one more pocket than there are rows.

Because every path is made of fifty-fifty turns, most balls collect near the centre and only a few drift all the way to an edge. Mathematicians call the layout a Galton board, and the shape it creates is a binomial distribution. The payout table flips that curve around: common centre pockets pay less, while the rare outer pockets pay much more. That is the logic behind casino thorfortune style boards as well.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Every pocket value is set roughly in line with its probability, then trimmed by a small house margin. Six words worth knowing

Pocket
A slot at the bottom of the board that holds the multiplier a finished ball lands on.
Rows
The number of peg lines a ball crosses; more rows mean more pockets and tighter odds.
Risk level
A preset that stretches or flattens the payout table without changing the long-run return very much.
House margin
The small gap between fair odds and paid odds that funds the operator.
Provably fair
A setup of published hashes that lets players check that a result was not changed.
Variance
The amount of swing around the average result — the thing the risk switch really changes.

Straight answers to five questions

Is every drop genuinely random?

On licensed platforms, yes. Each deflection comes from an audited random number generator, and provably fair versions let you confirm after the round that the path was fixed before the ball moved.

Do more rows make the game pay better?

No. Extra rows add pockets and stretch the odds curve, so edge prizes grow while their chance falls. The overall return stays broadly the same across board sizes.

Which risk level is the smart choice?

None is mathematically better. Low risk smooths the ride, while high risk concentrates value in rare edge hits. Choose based on how much swing your budget can handle.

Can I practise without spending anything?

Yes. The board on this page uses virtual credits, and most operators provide a free mode with the same odds. The maths does not change when real money is involved.

Does timing or button rhythm influence where a ball lands?

No. The path is decided by the random generator, not by when or how fast you press drop. Any pattern you notice is coincidence that feels meaningful.
